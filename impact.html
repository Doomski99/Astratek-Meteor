Effect of Impact Angle on Impacts

The angle of impact (measured from the horizontal) affects both the efficiency of energy transfer into the ground and the distribution of destructive effects.
Although the impact energy formula already includes the angle through the sinθ term (which reduces the total effective energy), the pattern of damage on the surface also changes significantly.

Steep Impact (60°–90°)

  • Energy coupling: maximum; almost all of the asteroid’s energy reaches the ground.
  • Crater: circular, symmetric ejecta blanket.
  • Blast & thermal: concentric and symmetric rings.
  • Interpretation: most intense local destruction.

Moderate Impact (30°–60°)

  • Most common impact angle range.
  • Energy coupling: high, only slightly reduced compared to vertical.
  • Crater: almost circular, small asymmetry in ejecta.
  • Blast & thermal: nearly symmetric.
  • Interpretation: usually taken as the reference scenario in studies.

Shallow Impact (15°–30°)

  • Energy coupling: reduced; part of the energy dissipates in the atmosphere.
  • Crater: smaller, often elongated; ejecta focused downrange.
  • Blast & thermal: elongated footprint, with stronger effects downrange.
  • Extreme case: if angle <15°, the asteroid may skip the atmosphere instead of striking the ground.

Scope (Asteroids): Minimal asteroid impacts. This is the smallest possible range of asteroids capable of reaching Earth’s surface without fully burning up in the atmosphere. Effects are limited to city scale, usually as an airburst shockwave or a very small crater, while smaller objects below this threshold disintegrate harmlessly before impact.

1- Blast Overpressure (Peak ΔP at ground)

  • The Analogy: The shockwave from a gigantic explosion. This is the force that physically knocks down buildings and trees.
  • Technical Specification: This is the peak overpressure in kilopascals (kPa) or pounds per square inch (psi) at ground level.
    • 100 kPa (~14.5 psi): Severe Structural Damage. Near-total building collapse. Radius: 0.5–1.5 km.
    • 35 kPa (~5 psi): Heavy Damage. Collapse of wood-frame buildings; serious injury threshold. Radius: 1–3 km.
    • 10 kPa (~1.5 psi): Light Damage. Widespread glass breakage. Radius: 5–15 km.

2- Thermal Radiation (Integrated fluence at ground)

  • The Analogy: The intense heat flash from a nuclear detonation. It instantly ignites materials and causes catastrophic burns.
  • Technical Specification: This is the total radiant energy deposited per unit area in kilojoules per square meter (kJ/m²).
    • 100 kJ/m²: Robust Ignition. Spontaneous ignition of wood, fabrics, and vegetation. Radius: ~1–2 km.
    • 20 kJ/m²: 3rd-Degree Burns & Fires. >99% probability of severe burns; ignition of thin fuels. Radius: ~1–3 km.
    • 2-10 kJ/m²: Painful Burns & Spot Fires. 2nd-degree burns; ignition of easily flammable materials. Radius: ~5–10 km.

3- Cratering (if ground strike occurs)

  • The Analogy: A large open-pit mine or a small reservoir suddenly appearing. This is the permanent scar of the impact.
  • Technical Specification: The final crater dimensions and ejecta blanket distribution.
    • Final Diameter: 150–600 m (upper outlier to ~800 m).
    • Depth: 10–70 m (typically a 1:4 to 1:10 depth-to-diameter ratio).
    • Ejecta Thickness: Decays rapidly from tens of cm at the rim to mere mm by ~2 km distance.

4- Seismic Equivalent

  • The Analogy: A noticeable, damaging earthquake centered directly on the impact point.
  • Technical Specification: The energy is expressed as a moment magnitude (Mw) and the shaking is described on the Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) scale.
    • Moment Magnitude (Mw): ~4.0–5.5 near source.
    • Intensity (MMI): VI-VII within ~5–15 km. This represents moderate to strong shaking: felt by all, furniture moves, and light damage occurs.

5- Ocean Impact / Tsunami (if over water)

  • The Analogy: A mega-tsunami generated by a sudden, massive displacement of water, like a submarine landslide.
  • Technical Specification: Wave height is highly dependent on water depth and distance from the impact.
    • Near-field Wave Height (within 20–50 km): ~5–20 m.
    • Coastal Run-up: Amplification upon hitting shorelines can easily exceed >10 m.
    • Far-field Wave Height (a few hundred km away): Typically decays to ≤0.5–1 m due to geometric spreading and energy dissipation.

6- Timing of Effects

The sequence of events is critical for understanding the rapid onset of destruction. The initial thermal flash is instantaneous.

  • Shock Front Arrival: The destructive blast wave travels at roughly the speed of sound (~340 m/s). For the radii in this range:

    • At the glass breakage radius (5–15 km): Arrives ~15–45 seconds after the flash.
    • At the heavy damage radius (1–3 km): Arrives ~3–9 seconds after the flash.
    • At the severe damage radius (0.5–1.5 km): Arrives ~1–4 seconds after the flash.

  • Ejecta Arrival: The fallout of coarse debris follows ballistic trajectories.

    • Widespread coarse ejecta arrives within ~10 to 75 seconds after the impact across the affected area.

  • Seismic Shaking: Felt almost instantly (within 1-3 seconds) across the entire zone due to the high speed of seismic waves.

impact consequences vary by angle while maintaining the same energy scale:

  • Moderate Angles (30°-60°): Balanced energy distribution with nearly circular effects
  • Shallow Angles (15°-30°): Reduced ground energy creates elongated damage corridors

Examples:

Note: Starting from Range B (Regional scale) and continuing through Range C (Continental scale) and Range D (Global/Planetary scale), the consequences of asteroid and comet impacts are essentially the same. At these higher energies, both types of bodies penetrate the atmosphere and deliver their energy to the surface, producing identical categories of effects: cratering, blast waves, thermal radiation, seismic shaking, tsunamis, and large-scale atmospheric disruption. The only meaningful difference remains in the smallest impacts (Range A), where comets are more likely to fragment in the atmosphere.

Range B - (Regional Scale)

Scope: Regional impacts. This energy range produces the same categories of effects as a smaller (city-scale) impact,(range A) but with dramatically increased intensity and geographic scale. The devastation can encompass an entire region or small country.

Blast Overpressure (Scaled up from city-level)

  • 100 kPa (Total Destruction): Radius expands to 2–5 km. Reinforced concrete buildings collapse.
  • 35 kPa (Heavy Damage): Radius expands to 5–15 km. Widespread structural failure and tree blowdown.
  • 10 kPa (Glass Breakage): Radius expands to 15–40 km. Universal window breakage and light damage.

Thermal Radiation (Scaled up from city-level)

  • 100 kJ/m² (Spontaneous Ignition): Radius expands to 2–4 km. Near-instant ignition of all combustibles.
  • 20 kJ/m² (3rd-Degree Burns): Radius expands to 10–20 km. Causes severe burns and ignites wildfires.
  • 2-10 kJ/m² (Painful Burns): Radius expands to 20–50 km. Causes secondary burns and spot fires over a vast area.

Crater Formation (if ground strike; scaled up from city-level)

  • Final Diameter: 1–5 km (previously 150–600 m).
  • Depth: 100–500 m (previously 10–70 m).
  • Ejecta Blanket: Debris is several meters thick at the rim (previously tens of cm), burying the immediate landscape.

Seismic Effects (Scaled up from city-level)

  • Equivalent Magnitude: Mw ~6.0–7.0 (previously Mw ~4.0–5.5).
  • Shaking Intensity: MMI VII–VIII (severe) within ~50–100 km (previously within ~5–15 km). Capable of triggering regional landslides.

Ocean Impact / Tsunami (if over water; scaled up from city-level)

  • Near-field Wave Height: 50–100 m (previously 5–20 m) within 50–100 km.
  • Coastal Run-up: >50 m possible (previously >10 m), causing catastrophic coastal flooding.
  • Far-field Waves: 5–10 m (previously ≤0.5–1 m), representing a major trans-oceanic hazard.

Atmospheric / Environmental Effects (New for this scale)

  • Dust & Aerosols: Significant material is injected into the stratosphere, a phenomenon not seen at lower energies.
  • Regional Climate Effects: Can cause short-term regional cooling, darkness, and potential acid rain, extending the disaster beyond the immediate blast zone.

Timing of Effects (Scaled up from city-level)

  • Shock Front Arrival: The destructive blast wave travels at roughly the speed of sound (~340 m/s). For the larger radii in this range:

    • At the glass breakage radius (15–40 km): Arrives ~45–120 seconds after the flash.
    • At the heavy damage radius (5–15 km): Arrives ~15–45 seconds after the flash.
    • At the total destruction radius (2–5 km): Arrives ~6–15 seconds after the flash.

  • Ejecta Arrival: The fallout of coarse debris follows ballistic trajectories.

    • Widespread coarse ejecta arrives within ~1 to 4 minutes after the impact across the affected region.

  • Seismic Shaking: Felt almost instantly (within 1-10 seconds) across the entire region due to the high speed of seismic waves.

  • Steep Angles (60°-90°): Maximum energy transfer creates symmetric circular regional devastation
  • Moderate Angles (30°-60°): Balanced energy distribution with nearly circular regional effects
  • Shallow Angles (15°-30°): Reduced ground energy creates elongated regional damage corridors

Famous Past Asteroid Impacts

Based on real impact events and scientific data, here are 2 famous regionally-scaled asteroids that fit Range B:

Asteroid:

1- Barringer Crater Impactor (Arizona, USA)

  • Actual Event: Created Meteor Crater in Arizona
  • Size: ~50 meter iron asteroid
  • Slammed into the desert, instantly blasting out a crater so massive and perfectly round that it looks like a giant, permanent thumbprint on the Earth's surface.
  • Anyone within miles would have been vaporized. Today, it's a stunning and humbling tourist attraction, a quiet desert landscape that hides a violent, instant creation story.

2- Sikhote-Alin Meteorite (Russia, 1947)

  • Actual Event: A massive iron meteorite impact in the Sikhote-Alin Mountains
  • Size: Estimated to be hundreds of tons before atmospheric entry
  • Exploded in a spectacular airburst, creating a shower of thousands of metal fragments that scarred the landscape with over 100 craters.
  • Eyewitnesses described a fireball brighter than the sun and a deafening series of explosions. The resulting "iron rain" left a vast field of extraterrestrial debris that is still collected by scientists and hunters today.

Comets:

1- Tunguska Event Impactor (Siberia, 1908)

  • Actual Event: 1908 Tunguska airburst over Siberia
  • Size: ~60-80 meter stony asteroid
  • Exploded in a massive airburst, flattening an entire forest the size of a major city. The force was so immense it knocked people off their feet hundreds of kilometers away.
  • Witnesses saw a fireball brighter than the sun, followed by a heat wave that burned skin and a shockwave that shattered windows. For nights after, the sky glowed eerily across the world, sparking doomsday fears.

2- The 2002 Eastern Mediterranean Event (not named)

  • Actual Event: A large airburst detected over the Mediterranean Sea
  • Size: Estimated ~10 meter comet or asteroid
  • Detonated high in the atmosphere with the energy of a nuclear bomb, releasing a powerful shockwave detected by satellites and seismic stations.
  • The event occurred luckily over the open sea. Had it happened over a populated coastal city, it could have caused widespread destruction and mass panic, serving as a stark modern warning of the impact threat.

Range C (Continental Scale)

Scope: Continental impacts. This energy range represents a global catastrophe, with immediate effects devastating an area the size of a continent and secondary environmental effects threatening worldwide disruption. The energy release is equivalent to thousands to millions of the largest nuclear weapons.

1- Blast Overpressure

  • 100 kPa (~14.5 psi): radius 10–20 km → complete destruction of all structures.
  • 35 kPa (~5 psi): radius 30–60 km → major urban areas leveled.
  • 10 kPa (~1.5 psi): radius 100–200 km → widespread glass break, structural damage.
  • Effect level: An entire nation-sized region is within destructive blast zones.

2- Thermal Radiation

  • 100 kJ/m²: radius 10–15 km → instant ignition of most fuels, unsurvivable burns.
  • 20 kJ/m²: radius 30–60 km → widespread fires, 3rd-degree burns to exposed people.
  • 2–10 kJ/m²: radius 100–200 km → ignition spots and mass casualties.
  • Effect level: Continental firestorms possible; entire forests and cities set alight.

3- Crater Formation (if ground strike)

  • Final diameter: 5–20 km.
  • Depth: 0.5–1.5 km.
  • Ejecta blanket:
    • Tens of m thick near rim.
    • 1–5 m thick within 10–20 km.
    • cm-scale deposits out to hundreds of km.
  • Effect level: Crater larger than major cities; ejecta fallout buries entire regions.

4- Seismic Effects

  • Equivalent earthquake magnitude: Mw ~7.0–8.0.
  • Intensity (MMI):
    • VIII–IX (severe to violent) within 100–300 km.
    • Triggering of massive landslides and fault ruptures.
  • Effect level: Comparable to the largest historical earthquakes, but focused at the impact site.

5- Ocean Impact / Tsunami (if over water)

  • Near-field waves: 100–300 m high within 100–200 km.
  • Coastal run-up: >100 m possible on nearby coasts.
  • Far-field waves: 10–30 m across ocean basins; destructive trans-oceanic tsunami possible.
  • Effect level: Multiple coastlines across oceans at risk.

6- Atmospheric / Environmental Effects

  • Dust and ejecta injected into stratosphere (10¹³–10¹⁵ kg).
  • Sunlight reduction for weeks–months at continental scale.
  • Regional cooling and short-term climate disruption.
  • Ozone destruction possible from NOx chemistry in upper atmosphere.
  • Effect level: Begins crossing from regional to global environmental hazard.

7- Timing of Effects (Scaled up from regional-level)

The sequence of events remains consistent but operates on dramatically extended timescales due to the continental-scale distances involved.

  • Shock Front Arrival: The destructive blast wave travels at roughly the speed of sound (~340 m/s). For the massive radii in this range:

    • At the widespread damage radius (100–200 km): Arrives ~5–10 minutes after the flash.
    • At the urban destruction radius (30–60 km): Arrives ~1.5–3 minutes after the flash.
    • At the total annihilation radius (10–20 km): Arrives ~30–60 seconds after the flash.

  • Ejecta Arrival: The fallout of coarse debris follows ballistic trajectories.

    • Widespread coarse ejecta arrives within ~5 to 15 minutes after the impact across the continental-scale affected area.

  • Seismic Shaking: Felt almost instantly (within 5-20 seconds) across the entire continent due to the high speed of seismic waves.

8- Rotation / Orbit Effects

  • No measurable effect on Earth’s orbit or rotation.
  • Energies still ~10 orders of magnitude below what is needed to perturb Earth’s dynamics.

impact consequences vary by angle while maintaining continental-scale energy:

  • Moderate Angles (30°-60°): Balanced continental effects with standard crater formation and widespread damage
  • Shallow Angles (15°-30°): Elongated continental destruction corridors with one-sided ejecta distribution and directional tsunamis

Range D — (global scale)

Scope: Global catastrophe. Comparable to the Chicxulub impact (66 Myr ago) that caused mass extinction. Asteroid size typically 1–5 km (stony) at ~15–20 km/s.

1- Blast Overpressure

  • 100 kPa (~14.5 psi): radius 50–100 km → complete destruction of all structures.
  • 35 kPa (~5 psi): radius 200–400 km → heavy building and forest destruction.
  • 10 kPa (~1.5 psi): radius 500–1000 km → widespread damage to infrastructure and vegetation.
  • Effect level: Multiple countries simultaneously devastated.

2- Thermal Radiation

  • 100 kJ/m²: radius 30–50 km → ignition of all combustibles, unsurvivable burns.
  • 20 kJ/m²: radius 200–400 km → widespread fires, 3rd-degree burns.
  • 2–10 kJ/m²: radius 1000+ km → spot ignition, severe burns.
  • Effect level: Firestorms across continental scales. Secondary ignition from re-entering ejecta may trigger global wildfires.

3- Crater Formation

  • Final diameter: 50–150 km (Chicxulub: ~180 km).
  • Depth: 3–5 km.
  • Ejecta blanket:
    • Hundreds of m thick near rim.
    • Tens of m thick at 50–100 km.
    • cm–m deposits spread globally.
  • Effect level: Crater the size of small seas; ejecta veil covers the planet.

4- Seismic Effects

  • Equivalent earthquake magnitude: Mw ~9.0–10.0.
  • Intensity (MMI):
    • IX–X (violent to extreme) within 1000 km.
    • Fault rupture, massive landslides globally possible.
  • Effect level: Comparable to the largest tectonic earthquakes ever recorded, but triggered by impact.

5- Ocean Impact / Tsunami

  • Near-field waves: >500 m high within 200–500 km.
  • Coastal run-up: hundreds of m possible on adjacent coasts.
  • Far-field waves: 10–100 m trans-oceanic tsunamis; destruction across multiple ocean basins.
  • Effect level: Entire ocean basins destabilized; global tsunami hazard.

6- Atmospheric & Global Environmental Effects

  • Dust & aerosols: 10¹⁵–10¹⁷ kg injected into stratosphere.
  • Sunlight blocked: 50–90% reduction for months to years.
  • Impact winter: global surface cooling by 10–20°C for 1–3 years.
  • Global fires: ejecta re-entry ignites biomass worldwide → injection of soot/CO₂.
  • Acid rain: vaporized rock + ocean salts produce nitric/sulfuric acids.
  • Effect level: Mass extinction driver: collapse of food chains, long-term biosphere disruption.

7- Rotation / Orbit Effects

Rotation/Orbit quick rules (apply everywhere in Range D)

  • Orbit: impact location does not matter. Earth ΔV is set by asteroid momentum direction; magnitude remains negligible.
  • Day length (ΔT): scale your equatorial best-case by latitude: ΔT(φ) ≈ ΔT_max · cos|φ|.
  • Tilt kick (Δε): scale by sin|φ| and by the component of momentum perpendicular to spin axis (max near poles, oblique approach).

Results for ΔT_max:

  • 1 km: ±0.003 ms
  • 3 km: ±0.080 ms
  • 5 km: ±0.37 ms

Note: signs: eastward (prograde) → shorter day, westward (retrograde) → longer day. Near-vertical strikes give minimal ΔT regardless of latitude.

Note: Both asteroids and comets can produce impacts up to Range D (global extinction scale). However, only extremely large asteroids — hundreds of kilometers across — are capable of reaching Range E. No known comet nucleus is large enough to deliver true Range E (planet-altering) energy; such collisions are comparable to the Mars-sized impactor believed to have formed the Moon.

Range E — Planetary Scale (>10²² J)

Scope: Impacts with energy exceeding 10²² Joules. This range encompasses a progression from the lower bound of planetary-scale disruption to the upper bound of literal planet-altering events. We subdivide this range to clarify the escalation of effects.

E1: Global Catastrophe / Near-Planet Killer (10²² – 10²³ J)

  • Impactor: ~5 km to ~10 km diameter (stony) at typical velocities.
  • Concept: This is the extreme upper end of a biospheric extinction event. It represents a more severe version of the Chicxulub impact, with darker, colder, and longer-lasting conditions that could push the biosphere to the brink of total collapse, with recovery taking tens of millions of years.

Famous Asteroids:

1- Chicxulub Impact (Yucatán, Mexico)

The Impact: A 10-15 kilometer wide asteroid struck Earth, creating the famous crater linked to the dinosaur extinction.

What Happened to Earth:

  • Created a 180-kilometer wide crater that is now buried under sediment.
  • Triggered global firestorms and massive tsunamis that swept across oceans.
  • Threw so much dust into the atmosphere that it blocked sunlight worldwide for years.

How Long It Took to Recover:

  • First few years: "Impact winter" with freezing temperatures and darkness.
  • Thousands of years: Climate slowly stabilized, but ecosystems remained simple.
  • Millions of years: New species evolved to replace the extinct dinosaurs, leading to the age of mammals.

Effects on Earth's Rotation and Orbit:

  • The impact caused a tiny, almost undetectable change in Earth's rotation.
  • No significant change to Earth's tilt or orbit around the Sun.
  • The planet's fundamental motion in space remained unchanged.

Unique Consequences:

  • Caused the extinction of 75% of all species, including most dinosaurs.
  • Created a worldwide layer of iridium-rich dust that scientists use to date the event.
  • Cleared the way for mammals to eventually dominate the planet.
  • The crater's unique structure influences local groundwater flow even today.

2- Popigai Impact (Siberia, Russia)

The Impact: An 8-kilometer wide asteroid hit Siberia, creating one of the best-preserved large craters on Earth.

What Happened to Earth:

  • Formed a 100-kilometer wide crater that remains clearly visible today.
  • Generated so much heat and pressure that it instantly transformed graphite into trillions of carats of diamonds.
  • Coincided with a major period of global cooling and ecosystem changes.

How Long It Took to Recover:

  • First century: Local area remained uninhabitable due to heat and debris.
  • Thousands of years: Crater lakes formed and basic ecosystems returned.
  • Millions of years: The crater became a unique geological feature, preserving evidence of the impact.

Effects on Earth's Rotation and Orbit:

  • Caused minimal, temporary effects on Earth's rotation.
  • No lasting changes to Earth's axial tilt or orbital characteristics.
  • The impact was significant locally but not globally disruptive to Earth's motion.

Unique Consequences:

  • Created the world's largest diamond field, though the diamonds are mostly industrial quality.
  • Provides one of the clearest records of a major impact in the geological timeline.
  • Helped scientists understand how impacts can create valuable mineral resources.
  • Serves as a natural laboratory for studying large impact processes.

E2: Planet Killer (10²³ – 10²⁵ J)

  • Impactor: ~10 km to ~50 km diameter.
  • Concept: This is a true planetary sterilization event. Recovery of complex life is impossible. The surface environment is reset to a pre-biotic state for hundreds of millions of years. The energy is sufficient to cause global crustal melting and significant atmosphere loss.

1- Vredefort Impact (South Africa)

The Impact: A massive asteroid about 15-20 kilometers wide slammed into Earth, creating the largest confirmed impact crater on our planet.

What Happened to Earth:

  • The impact created a crater 250-300 kilometers across - large enough to swallow several cities.
  • The explosion was more powerful than all nuclear weapons combined.
  • Rocks were thrown thousands of kilometers, and the heat generated was intense enough to melt the Earth's crust in the area.

How Long It Took to Recover:

  • First few years: The immediate area remained a molten wasteland.
  • Thousands of years: The crater filled with water and began to cool.
  • Millions of years: Erosion and geological processes slowly disguised the crater, though it remains visible today.

Effects on Earth's Rotation and Orbit:

  • The impact likely caused a tiny, temporary change in Earth's rotation speed.
  • It did not significantly alter Earth's tilt or orbit around the Sun.
  • Any changes were too small to have lasting effects on the planet's climate system.

Unique Consequences:

  • Created the largest verified impact structure on Earth.
  • Generated so much heat and pressure that it created unique rock formations.
  • If this happened today, it would cause complete planetary sterilization - ending all complex life.

2- Sudbury Impact (Canada)

The Impact: Another enormous asteroid, roughly 10-15 kilometers in diameter, struck Earth and formed one of the world's largest impact structures.

What Happened to Earth:

  • Created a massive crater originally 130-260 kilometers wide.
  • The impact was so powerful it melted deep parts of Earth's crust.
  • This melting created one of the world's richest nickel and copper deposits.

How Long It Took to Recover:

  • Initial millennia: The crater remained a deep basin filled with impact melt.
  • Millions of years: Mineral-rich fluids deposited valuable metals that we mine today.
  • 1.8 billion years later: The structure is still visible, though heavily modified by geological forces.

Effects on Earth's Rotation and Orbit:

  • Caused minor, temporary changes to Earth's rotation.
  • No permanent alteration to Earth's axial tilt or orbital path.
  • The impact was significant but not large enough to fundamentally change Earth's position in space.

Unique Consequences:

  • Created one of Earth's most valuable mining districts from the impact melt.
  • Helped scientists understand how large impacts can create economic mineral deposits.
  • Demonstrated that even planet-killing impacts can create resources that later benefit civilization.

E3: World-Former / Planetary Disruption (>10²⁵ J)

  • Impactor: >50 km diameter, up to planetary embryos (Mars-sized, ~1000s of km).
  • Concept: This is no longer an "impact" in the conventional sense; it's a planetary accretion or disruption event. It doesn't just kill the planet—it can remake it or destroy it. The Moon-forming impact (Theia) is the quintessential E3 event.

Famous Asteroid :

Theia Impact (The Moon-Formed Impact)

The Impact: A Mars-sized planet called Theia smashed into the early Earth. The collision was so powerful it melted both planets and blasted a huge amount of rock into orbit around Earth.

What Changed on Earth:

  • Earth got bigger, gaining about 10% of its current mass.
  • The collision completely melted Earth's surface, creating a global ocean of magma.
  • Material from the impact eventually clumped together to form our Moon.

How Long It Took to Recover:

  • First 100 years: Earth was a ball of molten rock with surface temperatures over 2000°C.
  • Next 10,000 years: The surface began to cool and solidify; a new atmosphere started to form.
  • 1 million years later: The first oceans appeared as water vapor condensed.
  • 100 million years later: Earth's crust finally stabilized, creating conditions where life could eventually begin.

How Earth's Spin and Orbit Changed:

  • Earth's day lengthened from 5 hours to 18 hours.
  • The impact gave Earth its tilt (23.5 degrees), which creates our seasons.
  • Earth's orbit around the Sun became more circular and stable.

Special Effects of This Impact:

  • Created our Moon, which helps stabilize Earth's climate.
  • Gave Earth a larger iron core, strengthening our magnetic field.
  • Completely reshaped Earth's surface and interior structure.
  • Made Earth unique among planets in our solar system.
